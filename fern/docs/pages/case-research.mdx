The case corpus is being converted from a simple list into structured records. Until an underlying decision has been verified against an authoritative source, summaries are treated as index material rather than as authoritative statements of law.
</Callout>

## Find a case

Use the site search for a case name, citation, statute, rule, court, or research topic. Case records will progressively expose structured metadata and relationships as the corpus is verified.

## Verification states

**Verified primary source** means the record has been checked against the underlying decision or an authoritative repository.

**Secondary-indexed** means the record is derived from an existing compilation and still requires primary-source verification.

**Unresolved** means a material field or relationship could not yet be established reliably.

The interface should make these states visible rather than presenting every record with identical confidence.

## The case graph

`CASE` → `STATUTE`

`CASE` → `RULE`

`CASE` → `CASE`

`CASE` → `BILL`

`CASE` → `DISSENT`

This graph will later power related-case panels, citation maps, statutory interpretation trails, legislative-history links, and source-grounded AI retrieval.

## Research discipline

A summary is not a holding. A citation is not proof that a proposition was actually decided. Similarity between cases is not precedent. A dissent is not controlling law. The data model therefore keeps source material, extracted metadata, legal propositions, and analytical conclusions distinct.
