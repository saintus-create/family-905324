## What can connect to what

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Cases">
    A verified case can connect to statutes and rules it interprets or applies, other cases it cites or that cite it, legislation affecting the legal framework, relevant people, empirical evidence, and structured arguments.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Legislation">
    A bill can connect to the statutes it proposes to amend, related or predecessor bills, committee analyses, votes, chaptered acts, cases interpreting the resulting law, and evidence discussed during enactment.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Evidence">
    Evidence can connect to the legal propositions it informs, cases discussing the underlying issue, legislation relying on or discussing the research, researchers and institutions, and arguments that use or challenge the finding.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="People">
    A person can connect to cases, legislation, publications, institutions, and research only where the relationship is documented by an appropriate source.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Relationship language

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Cites / interprets / applies">These describe judicial or legal-authority relationships and should be supported by the underlying text.</Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Amends / repeals / implements">These describe legislative or regulatory changes and should point to an official legislative or regulatory source.</Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Supported by / contradicted by / criticized by">These describe analytical relationships and must identify the proposition being evaluated.</Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Parallel to / historical precursor">These describe comparative or historical relationships and should identify the basis for the comparison.</Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Every relationship has a confidence state

`Verified` means the relationship is supported by a checked source.

`Indexed` means it comes from an existing index and still needs primary verification.

`Unresolved` means the relationship is plausible or suspected but not established.

`Analytical` means it is an interpretation produced by research or analysis, not a source-level fact.

## Related research should remain visible

Individual pages should expose a compact **Related** region rather than forcing users to navigate back through the sidebar. A case can therefore surface its authorities, legislation, evidence, people, and related cases in context. A study can surface relevant cases and arguments. A bill can surface its predecessor, successor, resulting statute, and judicial treatment.

The result should feel like one connected library, while preserving the distinction between source material and interpretation.
