## Source-first architecture

The analytical layer should never replace the underlying authority. A future analysis record should preserve the source document, citation, extraction, analytical operation, and resulting inference as separate objects.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Retrieve">
    Identify the statute, rule, case, filing, study, or dataset.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Structure">
    Connect the source to its jurisdiction, date, procedural posture, subject matter, and related authorities.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Analyze">
    Apply legal, empirical, rhetorical, or computational methods to the structured material.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify">
    Trace every substantive conclusion back to its underlying source material.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Future research modules

| Module | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Case corpus | Import and normalize California appellate and trial decisions where legally available |
| Citation graph | Connect cases, statutes, rules, and authorities through citations and references |
| Procedural graph | Model events, filings, hearings, orders, deadlines, and procedural dependencies |
| Statistics | Preserve datasets and make empirical claims reproducible |
| Argument mapping | Represent claims, reasons, warrants, objections, rebuttals, and burdens |
| Rhetorical analysis | Identify framing, presupposition, narrative structure, attribution, and other linguistic features |
| Computational dialectics | Model conflicting positions, commitments, attacks, defenses, and resolution states |
| AI research assistant | Generate source-grounded summaries and analyses while preserving provenance and uncertainty |

## A rule for the analytical system

**Analysis is an additional layer, not a substitute for authority.**

The system should distinguish what a source says, what an analyst infers from it, what an empirical dataset demonstrates, and what an AI model proposes. Those categories must never silently collapse into one another.
