The Defense Toolkit is modeled as a relationship graph rather than a flat list of defenses. The underlying corpus contains structured records for six research pathways and explicit authority relationships.

## The research path

**Question → Proposition → Authority → Counterauthority → Procedure → Form → Evidence**

A relationship is not itself proof that a proposition is correct. It records how the corpus currently connects two research objects and preserves the source-review status of the proposition.

## Relationship types

| Relationship | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `supports` | The linked authority or evidence supports the proposition being researched. |
| `contradicts` | The linked material points against the proposition. |
| `distinguishes` | The linked authority may be materially different on facts, posture, or rule. |
| `limits` | The linked authority narrows the proposition. |
| `amends` | The linked source changes the legal text or rule being researched. |
| `interprets` | The linked authority supplies an interpretation of the proposition or source. |
| `implements` | A procedural rule or form operationalizes the legal requirement. |

## Why the graph preserves uncertainty

The corpus intentionally distinguishes **verified**, **source-derived**, **needs-current-law-review**, **disputed**, **superseded**, and **historical** material. A source can therefore be connected to a proposition without the connection being represented as an endorsement of the proposition's legal accuracy.

That distinction is essential for the Defense Toolkit because a research page should not silently transform an inherited characterization of a case into authoritative current California law.

## Underlying data

The structured records are stored in `fern/data/legal/defense-toolkit.records.json`; the relationship definitions are stored in `fern/data/legal/defense-toolkit.relationships.json`; and their structures are constrained by the corresponding schemas.
