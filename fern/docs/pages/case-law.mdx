## A

**Ashby v. Ashby**, 68 Cal.App.5th 491 (4th Dist., Div. 3, 2021) (2021), California Court of Appeal

Abusive litigation behavior and violations of court orders relating to support, fees, and custody should be considered when deciding whether to renew a DVRO.

## B

**Bianka M. v. Superior Court**, 5 Cal.5th 1004 (2018), California Supreme Court

Family courts need not require an absent parent to appear in court to issue Special Immigrant Juvenile Status findings, so long as the minor properly served that parent.

**Brubaker v. Strum**, citation not available (2022), California Court of Appeal

Clarifies for the first time that labelling abuse as "situational" is not a legal reason to require proof of new abuse in order to renew a domestic violence restraining order.

**Burquet v. Brumbaugh**, 223 Cal.App.4th 1140 (2014), California Court of Appeal

First case to interpret "disturbing the peace" as defined in the Domestic Violence Prevention Act in the context of a non-marital relationship.

## C

**Celia S. v. Hugo H.**, 3 Cal.App.5th 655 (2016), California Court of Appeal

The Family Code section 3044 presumption against joint custody remains in effect for 5 years, even if the restraining order against the abuser has expired.

**Christina L. v. Chauncey B.**, 229 Cal.App.4th 731 (2014), California Court of Appeal

The trial court is obligated to apply Family Code section 3044 when there was a recent restraining order issued against a parent.

**Colchester v. Lazaro**, 16 F.4th 712 (2021), U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit

Trial court must independently support its reasons for granting a Hague Convention return petition and allow the parent opposing return a fair opportunity to develop grave-risk evidence.

**Cueto v. Dozier**, 241 Cal.App.4th 550 (2015), California Court of Appeal

A DVRO renewal cannot be denied for lack of "reasonable apprehension of future abuse" where there is a documented history of physical abuse.

## D

**De la Luz Perez v. Torres-Hernandez**, 1 Cal.App.5th 389 (2016), California Court of Appeal

Evidence of abuse against the protected party's child is relevant to a DVRO renewal decision; fear of future physical abuse is not required for renewal.

## E

**Ellis v. Lyons**, 2 Cal.App.5th 404 (2016), California Court of Appeal

An out-of-state court's finding of domestic violence triggers the Family Code section 3044 presumption against granting custody to an abuser.

**Elmassian v. Flores**, citation not available (2021), California Court of Appeal

A tenant may assert the domestic-violence eviction defense even when non-domestic-violence grounds are also asserted; police reports need not name the perpetrator to satisfy the documentation requirement.

**Eneaji v. Ubboe**, 229 Cal.App.4th 1457 (2014), California Court of Appeal

A DVRO renewal does not require fear of future physical abuse specifically: fear of any abuse defined by the DVPA is sufficient.

## F

**Faton v. Ahmedo**, 236 Cal.App.4th 1160 (2015), California Court of Appeal

A request for attorney's fees is not barred for having been omitted from the initial DVRO application, and need not be decided together with the DVRO petition.

## G

**Garcia v. Escobar**, 17 Cal.App.5th 267 (2017), California Court of Appeal

A family court can renew a DVRO originally issued by a juvenile court after the juvenile case is dismissed, under Family Code section 6345.

**Gou v. Xiao**, 228 Cal.App.4th 812 (2014), California Court of Appeal

A parent may seek a restraining order based on abuse against their child, since child abuse can disturb the requesting parent's own peace.

## H

**Hogue v. Hogue**, 16 Cal.App.5th 833 (2017), California Court of Appeal

California courts have jurisdiction to issue a restraining order against an out-of-state person who commits an act of domestic violence against a California resident.

## I

**In re Bruno M.**, 28 Cal.App.5th 990 (2018), California Court of Appeal

A child witnessing domestic violence can have their mental and emotional peace disturbed, justifying a restraining order under the Welfare and Institutions Code.

**In re J.M.**, 50 Cal.App.5th 833 (2020), California Court of Appeal

A parent's delayed recognition of their own victimization does not mean it is not currently in the child's best interest to be returned to that parent.

**In re Jonathan B**, 235 Cal.App.4th 115 (2015), California Court of Appeal

A mother who immediately reported abuse to police did not "fail to protect" her children where the father's violence was not foreseeable.

**In re M.W.**, 238 Cal.App.4th 1444 (2015), California Court of Appeal

A non-abusive parent who declines to obtain a restraining order should not be charged with "failure to protect" their child.

**In re Marriage of Carlisle**, citation not available (2021), California Court of Appeal

Trial courts have authority to renew a DVRO while the original order is being appealed.

**In re Marriage of Davila**, 29 Cal.App.5th 220 (2018), California Court of Appeal

Testimony outside the written DVRO application may be considered where the application included general statements of threatened physical harm.

**In re Marriage of Emilie D.L.M. and Carlos C**, citation not available (2021), California Court of Appeal

An abusive parent's denial of their domestic violence problem forecloses ameliorative measures that would otherwise mitigate grave risk of harm under the Hague Convention.

**In re Marriage of Evilsizor and Sweeney**, 237 Cal.App.4th 1416 (2015), California Court of Appeal

Physical abuse is not necessary to issue a restraining order; disclosing intimate details of someone's life can constitute abuse; abusive speech is not protected by the First Amendment.

**In re Marriage of Fajota**, 230 Cal.App.4th 1487 (2014), California Court of Appeal

The Family Code section 3044 presumption against joint custody must be applied every time custody is considered, even for temporary orders, until rebutted.

**In re Marriage of Fregoso and Hernandez**, 5 Cal.App.5th 698 (2016), California Court of Appeal

A restraining order may issue even after a brief period of reconciliation; a single witness's testimony can be sufficient evidence.

**In re Marriage of J.Q. and T.B.**, 223 Cal.App.4th 687 (2014), California Court of Appeal

Spousal support can be awarded in a domestic violence action before a finding of domestic violence has been made.

**In re Marriage of Kumar**, 13 Cal.App.5th 1072 (2017), California Court of Appeal

An immigrant sponsor enforcing an I-864 Affidavit of Support is under no duty to mitigate damages by seeking full-time work.

**Isidora M. v. Silvino M**, 239 Cal.App.4th 11 (2015), California Court of Appeal

Trial courts may issue mutual DVROs only if both parties have filed their own requests for a restraining order.

## J

**J.J. v. M.F.**, 223 Cal.App.4th 968 (2014), California Court of Appeal

First decision to define "primary aggressor" in the context of mutual restraining orders, requiring consideration of the full relationship context.

**Jaime G. v. H.L.**, 25 Cal.App.5th 794 (2018), California Court of Appeal

A court cannot award any custody to a domestic abuser without written or on-the-record findings on each of the seven Family Code section 3044 rebuttal factors.

**Jameson v. Desta**, 5 Cal.5th 594 (2018), California Supreme Court

Counties must provide free court reporters to low-income litigants with fee waivers who request one; a policy with no fee-waiver exception is invalid.

## K

**K.L. v. R.H.**, 70 Cal.App.5th 965 (4th Dist., Div. 3, 2021) (2021), California Court of Appeal

Trial courts must weigh both parties' conduct against each other to identify the primary aggressor rather than considering allegations in isolation.

**Keisha W. v. Marvin M**, 229 Cal.App.4th 581 (2014), California Court of Appeal

Under the UCCJEA, California trial courts can modify another state's custody order at the request of a survivor who fled to California.

## L

**Lister v. Bowen**, 215 Cal.App.4th 319 (2013), California Court of Appeal

Any violation of a restraining order, including a non-violent one, is significant support for renewal.

## M

**McCord v. Smith**, citation not available (2020), California Court of Appeal

Courts must consider the totality of the circumstances; seemingly isolated acts of coercive control can together justify a restraining order.

**Melissa G. v. Raymond M.**, 27 Cal.App.5th 360 (2018), California Court of Appeal

A mutual restraining order requires factual findings under the Family Code; restraining orders based on separate incidents are still mutual restraining orders.

## N

**N.T. v. H.T.**, 34 Cal.App.5th 595 (2019), California Court of Appeal

Violation of a temporary restraining order constitutes abuse under Family Code section 6203(a)(4) and section 6320.

**Nevarez v. Tonna**, 227 Cal.App.4th 774 (2014), California Court of Appeal

A restraining order requires only a finding of a past act of abuse, not a finding of likely future abuse.

**Nicole G. v. Braithwaite**, 49 Cal.App.5th 990 (2020), California Court of Appeal

A survivor who moved out to flee abuse should not have to permanently vacate the residence; a move-out order can still issue against the abuser.

**Noergaard v. Noergaard**, 244 Cal.App.4th 76 (2015), California Court of Appeal

In an international child abduction case alleging grave risk, the trial court must hold a fair hearing considering all relevant abuse evidence.

## P

**Phillips v. Campbell**, 2 Cal.App.5th 844 (2016), California Court of Appeal

First case to define "dating relationship" under the DVPA; frequent intimate association characterized by expectation of affection qualifies.

**Priscila N. v. Leonardo G.**, 17 Cal.App.5th 1208 (2017), California Court of Appeal

A restraining order issued by a juvenile court is treated as "issued" under the DVPA for renewal purposes, extending Garcia v. Escobar.

## R

**Rodriguez v. Menjivar**, 243 Cal.App.4th 816 (2015), California Court of Appeal

Controlling and coercive behavior counts as abuse under the DVPA; time elapsed since abuse is not a basis to deny a DVRO.

**Rybolt v. Riley**, 20 Cal.App.5th 864 (2018), California Court of Appeal

Threatening or controlling conduct during shared parenting time is "abuse" under the DVPA and can support DVRO renewal.

## S

**S.Y. v. Superior Court**, 29 Cal.App.5th 324 (2018), California Court of Appeal

English language fluency cannot be used as a factor in awarding child custody.

**Sabato v. Brooks**, 242 Cal.App.4th 715 (2015), California Court of Appeal

Unwanted and harassing contacts, without allegations of violence or threats, are sufficient to issue a three-year DVRO.

## T

**Tanguilig v. Valdez**, 36 Cal.App.5th 514 (2019), California Court of Appeal

Good cause supported adding additional household members to a restraining order where the repeated abuse occurred outside the home.

## V

**Vinson v. Kinsey**, citation not available (2023), California Court of Appeal

A survivor's ongoing contact with an abusive ex-partner does not negate the abuse; threats need not be threats of violence to constitute abuse.
