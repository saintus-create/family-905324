## Evidence status

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Verified source">
    The underlying source has been checked and its provenance is recorded.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Primary dataset">
    The record points to the original dataset or official statistical source.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Peer reviewed">
    The work has undergone peer review as represented by the source record.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Secondary analysis">
    The record analyzes or summarizes another source rather than constituting the original evidence.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Needs review">
    A material source, methodology, or provenance question remains unresolved.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## How to read a study

<Steps>
  <Step title="Question">What was the research actually asking?</Step>
  <Step title="Design">How was the question investigated?</Step>
  <Step title="Population">Who or what was studied, and how were observations selected?</Step>
  <Step title="Measurement">How were concepts and outcomes operationalized?</Step>
  <Step title="Result">What did the study actually find?</Step>
  <Step title="Limitations">What alternative explanations, biases, or scope limits remain?</Step>
  <Step title="Legal relevance">How, if at all, does the evidence bear on a legal proposition?</Step>
</Steps>

## Evidence and legal reasoning

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Finding is not causation">
    An observed association does not by itself establish that one variable caused another.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Statistical significance is not practical importance">
    A statistically detectable result can still be small, imprecise, or legally irrelevant.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="A study does not become legal authority">
    Empirical research may inform policy or factual propositions while remaining distinct from statutes, regulations, and judicial holdings.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Contradictory evidence matters">
    The corpus should preserve competing findings rather than selecting only studies that support a preferred proposition.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Cross-reference model

A verified evidence record can connect to:

- **Cases** that discuss the relevant factual or empirical proposition
- **Statutes and regulations** governing the legal question
- **Bills and legislative analyses** that rely upon or discuss empirical material
- **People and institutions** responsible for the research
- **Historical records** that establish temporal context
- **Arguments** that use the evidence as a premise
- **Counterarguments** challenging its interpretation or applicability

Each connection should identify its source and relationship type rather than relying on keyword similarity alone.

## Visual research rule

Charts, archival images, maps, and diagrams belong here when they communicate evidence or context. Decorative imagery should not compete with the research. Every substantive figure should have a caption, provenance, and enough context to understand what it represents.
