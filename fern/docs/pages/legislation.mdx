## A legislative lineage model

<Steps>
  <Step title="Current provision">
    Start with the current Family Code section or Rule of Court provision.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enacting measure">
    Identify the bill, chapter, session, and effective date that established the provision.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Amendment history">
    Follow every material amendment, repeal, reenactment, renumbering, or substantive revision.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Related measures">
    Connect companion, competing, successor, and adjacent bills.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Parallel jurisdictions">
    Compare similar statutory mechanisms across states while preserving differences in definitions, procedure, and legal effect.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Historical context">
    Trace earlier acts, model legislation, judicial developments, social conditions, and institutional changes where reliable sources establish the relationship.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Why the distinction matters

Legislative history can provide context, but it should never be silently converted into a claim about statutory meaning. The research system therefore keeps the enacted text, legislative record, historical evidence, judicial interpretation, and analyst inference as distinct source layers.

A future record may show:

`Family Code § X`
→ `enacting bill`
→ `committee amendments`
→ `chaptered act`
→ `later amendments`
→ `related California bills`
→ `parallel state acts`
→ `historical precursor`
→ `cases interpreting the provision`

## National comparison

The national layer should not become a collection of superficial fifty-state summaries. Each parallel act should be mapped by:

- jurisdiction
- legislative session
- legal mechanism
- population covered
- definitions
- procedural requirements
- remedies
- effective date
- amendment history
- relationship to model legislation or prior acts
- relevant judicial interpretation

This makes cross-jurisdictional comparison analytically useful rather than merely decorative.

## Provenance rule

Every legislative-history relationship should have a source. A relationship may be classified as **documented**, **probable**, **analytical**, or **unresolved**. The system should never present an inferred legislative lineage as an established historical fact.
