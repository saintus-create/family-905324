## Civil-rights timeline

The eventual dataset should permit a researcher to move in both directions:

`historical condition`
→ `movement / proposal`
→ `bill`
→ `enactment or failure`
→ `litigation`
→ `judicial holding or dissent`
→ `constitutional development`
→ `subsequent legislation`
→ `current law`

A timeline should never imply that legal change was inevitable. Failed legislation, dissents, rejected arguments, and periods of retrenchment are part of the record too.

## LGBTQ family-law index

The index is intended to cover, among other subjects:

- marriage and marriage recognition
- same-sex marriage litigation
- domestic partnerships
- parentage and presumed parentage
- assisted reproduction
- adoption and foster care
- second-parent and stepparent adoption
- custody and visitation
- transgender and gender-nonconforming parents
- gender-marker and identity documentation
- anti-discrimination protections
- religious-liberty conflicts affecting family law
- constitutional challenges
- federal-state conflicts
- military and federal-benefit developments affecting families

Each subject should retain the distinction between enacted law, judicial interpretation, administrative policy, litigation position, dissent, and advocacy or commentary.

## Dissent as data

Judicial dissents are not controlling law, but they are valuable research objects. The database should preserve:

- author
- court and date
- case
- majority disposition
- issue of disagreement
- legal authorities relied upon
- competing rule or test proposed
- subsequent treatment
- whether later courts adopted, distinguished, or rejected the reasoning

This permits a researcher to study how legal arguments move through time instead of treating each opinion as an isolated document.

## Progress and retrenchment

The system should avoid assuming that legal history is a simple progression toward the present. For each major development, researchers should be able to identify expansion, limitation, reversal, delay, non-enactment, and later restoration or modification.

## Federal and state interaction

Federal developments should be connected to California law without collapsing the jurisdictions. Relevant relationships may include:

`federal constitutional decision`
→ `California response`
→ `state legislation`
→ `administrative implementation`
→ `subsequent litigation`

The same model should work in reverse when California develops a rule that later becomes part of a broader national comparison.

## Provenance requirement

Every historical relationship should identify its source and type. A database relationship is not itself historical proof. The system should distinguish documented chronology from an analyst's interpretation of influence, causation, or ideological lineage.
